# Focus-product comparison

Research date: July 25, 2026.

| Product | Strongest idea | Useful in Still | Deliberately left out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [one sec](https://one-sec.app/) | Adds a mindful interruption before habitual app or website use; supports intentions and browser interventions | Eight-second breathing pause, intention capture, short intentional access | Subscription, cross-device account, and a long intervention menu |
| [1Focus](https://onefocusapp.com/) | Scheduled, difficult-to-bypass blocking with multiple lists and allowlists | Strict timed focus sessions and a clear protected-site list | Native-app and whole-internet blocking, which a Chrome extension cannot safely provide |
| [Regain](https://regainapp.co/) | Device-local screen-time control with a simple blocking experience | Local-only stats and a low-friction setup | Mobile-only controls and attention-heavy analytics |
| [Forest](https://www.forestapp.cc/) | Makes focused time tangible through a growing tree and a visual history | A calm breathing seed/orb and the idea of growth over punishment | Coins, collectibles, streak pressure, social rooms, and killing a tree when the user slips |

## Product position

Still is not only a timer or only a blocker. It uses the lightest intervention that can work:

1. **Pause** habitual visits outside a focus session.
2. **Choose** by naming an intention or returning to the previous task.
3. **Protect** a timed session when stronger boundaries are useful.
4. **Reflect** through small, local-only totals instead of a guilt-heavy dashboard.

## Chrome-extension boundary

Still can protect websites in Chrome. It cannot block native Mac/Windows apps, silence phone notifications, or enforce restrictions in other browsers. Those require an operating-system app or managed-device policy.
